The family tree

The important split is neoplastic germ-cell differentiation versus developmental epithelial inclusion.

Neoplasm

Primitive germ cell → germ-cell tumor

Normal germ cells are destined for the gonads. Germ-cell tumors occur in the gonads and at characteristic extragonadal sites, especially midline locations.
Primitive phenotypeGerminoma
CNS name. Testicular analogue = seminoma; ovarian analogue = dysgerminoma.
DifferentiationTeratoma
Germ cells differentiate into recognizable tissues: fat, skin, bone, cartilage, neural or GI/respiratory epithelium.
NGGCTYolk sac tumor
Important serum marker association: AFP elevation.
NGGCTEmbryonal / choriocarcinoma / mixed
Other nongerminomatous germ-cell tumors; choriocarcinoma is strongly associated with β-hCG.
Developmental inclusion

Trapped ectoderm → inclusion cyst

Ectoderm gets trapped along embryologic fusion planes. These are not the same biological process as germ-cell tumors.
KeratinEpidermoid
Squamous epithelial lining + keratin debris. Intracranially it looks nearly like CSF but restricts diffusion.
Skin + appendagesTrue dermoid cyst
Skin-like lining with hair follicles/sebaceous glands → hair, sebum and macroscopic fat.
Cutaneous cousinEpidermal inclusion cyst
Keratin-filled skin cyst. “Sebaceous cyst” is often an imprecise name.
Temporal bone cousinCholesteatoma
Keratinizing squamous epithelium in the middle ear/mastoid; diffusion restriction is highly useful.
The giant terminology trap: an ovarian “dermoid cyst” is a mature cystic teratoma and therefore a true germ-cell tumor. An intracranial dermoid is a developmental ectodermal inclusion cyst. Same nickname; different pathogenesis.

Teratoma: why it looks like “everything”

The imaging heterogeneity is the pathology.

EctodermSkin, hair, neural tissue
One reason teratomas and dermoids can superficially resemble one another.
MesodermFat, bone, cartilage, muscle
Macroscopic fat plus calcification/teeth strongly suggests mature teratomatous tissue in the right setting.
EndodermGI / respiratory epithelium
This is the key conceptual reason a teratoma is broader than merely “skin trapped somewhere.”
Mature vs immatureDifferentiation matters
Mature teratoma contains well-differentiated adult-type tissue; immature teratoma contains immature embryonal tissue, classically immature neuroepithelium.

The one-sentence mental models

Germinoma

Primitive germ-cell tumor with a CNS predilection for pineal and suprasellar regions.

Teratoma

Germ-cell tumor making recognizable body tissues. Expect a heterogeneous mixture.

True dermoid

Misplaced skin. Skin appendages generate fat/sebum/hair.

Epidermoid

Misplaced keratinizing squamous epithelium. Think keratin and DWI restriction.

Germinoma

CNS

  • Pineal region: classic, especially in boys
  • Suprasellar / infundibular: DI, pituitary dysfunction, visual symptoms
  • Bifocal pineal + suprasellar disease is characteristic
  • Less common: basal ganglia/thalamus; rare other CNS sites
  • Can disseminate through CSF pathways
Germ-cell tumors

Whole body

  • Testis and ovary
  • Anterior mediastinum
  • Retroperitoneum
  • Sacrococcygeal region
  • CNS, especially pineal/suprasellar
Teratoma

Characteristic sites

  • Ovary: mature cystic teratoma (“dermoid”)
  • Testis
  • Sacrococcygeal region in neonates/children
  • Anterior mediastinum
  • Retroperitoneum
  • CNS, usually pineal when intracranial
Epidermoid

Intracranial / skull / spine

  • CPA cistern: classic
  • Prepontine, parasellar, suprasellar and other basal cisterns
  • Quadrigeminal cistern / middle cranial fossa
  • Rare intraventricular lesions
  • Intradiploic skull and spinal canal lesions also occur
True dermoid

Fusion planes

  • Intracranial midline/paramidline: posterior fossa, suprasellar/parasellar, frontonasal regions
  • Head/neck: lateral eyebrow, nasal midline, floor of mouth, submental region, scalp
  • Spinal: especially lumbosacral; may accompany dysraphism or dermal sinus
Cholesteatoma

Temporal bone

  • Middle ear and mastoid
  • Acquired disease is common; congenital lesions also occur
  • Non-EPI DWI is particularly useful for small/residual disease
Epidermal inclusion cyst

Skin / subcutaneous tissues

  • Scalp, face, neck, trunk and extremities
  • Can occur after epidermal implantation from trauma/surgery
  • Filled with keratin rather than true sebaceous material
Neurenteric cyst

Endodermal developmental cyst

  • Ventral to brainstem
  • Craniocervical junction
  • Ventral spinal canal
  • Signal varies with proteinaceous content
Rathke cleft cyst

Rathke pouch remnant

  • Sellar and/or suprasellar
  • Often nonenhancing or thin rim enhancement
  • Signal varies with proteinaceous/mucoid content

Nomenclature trap room

These are the word pairs most likely to make you build the wrong disease model.

Ovarian dermoid ≠ true CNS dermoid

Ovarian “dermoid” = mature cystic teratoma = germ-cell tumor.

Intracranial dermoid = developmental ectodermal inclusion cyst.

Germinoma ↔ seminoma ↔ dysgerminoma

Closely related primitive germ-cell tumors named by site: CNS → germinoma; testis → seminoma; ovary → dysgerminoma.

Dermoid vs epidermoid

Dermoid: skin appendages → fat/hair/sebum.

Epidermoid: keratin only → near-CSF appearance with striking DWI restriction.

Epidermoid vs arachnoid cyst

Both may look CSF-like. Epidermoid usually has “dirty” FLAIR and restriction; arachnoid cyst follows CSF and does not restrict.

Epidermoid vs cholesteatoma

Both are keratinizing squamous epithelial lesions that can restrict diffusion. Geography usually separates them: intracranial cisterns versus middle ear/mastoid.

“Sebaceous cyst”

Many lesions casually called sebaceous cysts are actually epidermal inclusion cysts filled primarily with keratin.

Teratoma vs dermoid

A true dermoid essentially makes skin-related material. A teratoma can differentiate across germ layers, so bone/cartilage/GI/neural tissue can join fat and skin.

Rathke cyst vs craniopharyngioma

Both occur sellar/suprasellar and relate to Rathke pouch development, but Rathke cleft cyst is a developmental remnant cyst; craniopharyngioma is a neoplasm.
